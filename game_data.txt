=========================================
CRICKET
=========================================
Cricket matches are historically played on circular or oval grass fields called grounds.
The rectangular strip in the middle measuring exactly 22 yards is known as the pitch.
A standard cricket ball is constructed from solid cork covered with stitched red or white leather.
The primary tool for a batter is a flat-fronted wooden bat traditionally crafted from willow.
Wickets consist of three vertical wooden stumps hammered firmly into the ground.
Two small horizontal wooden pieces called bails rest safely atop the three vertical stumps.
A bowler must deliver six valid consecutive balls to complete a standard unit called an over.
Fielding teams are strictly limited to having eleven active players on the field at one time.
The wicketkeeper stands directly behind the batting stumps to catch missed deliveries.
An umpire signals a boundary four when a struck ball hits the ground and crosses the outer rope.
A clear boundary six is awarded when the ball clears the boundary rope completely on the fly.
Batters must cross and touch the ground behind the crease to record a legitimate run.
A dismissal by being bowled occurs when a legal delivery strikes the stumps and dislodges the bails.
The leg before wicket rule penalizes a batter if the ball hits their leg in line with the stumps.
A run out happens when fielders break the wicket with the ball while a batter is running outside the crease.
Stumping occurs when the wicketkeeper dislodges the bails while the batter is stepped out of their crease.
The International Cricket Council serves as the supreme global governing body for the sport.
Test cricket represents the ultimate endurance format spanning up to five consecutive days of play.
One Day International matches restrict each team to a maximum allocation of fifty overs.
Twenty20 cricket is an explosive short format where each side receives exactly twenty overs to bat.

BADMINTON
=========================================
Badminton is a high-speed racket sport played exclusively on a rectangular indoor court.
The game utilizes a highly aerodynamic open-coned lightweight object called a shuttlecock.
Shuttlecocks are traditionally constructed using sixteen overlapping feathers fixed to a cork base.
Players use lightweight carbon fiber or titanium composite stringed rackets to strike.
The net is suspended across the center of the court at a standard height of five feet.
Points can only be scored when the shuttlecock is successfully struck through the air.
Allowing the shuttlecock to touch the floor results in an immediate loss of rally.
A standard professional match is contested as a best-of-three games series structure.
Each individual game is played until a side reaches twenty-one points with a margin.
Serves must be delivered using an underhand motion below the server's waistline level.
The serve must travel diagonally across the net into the opponent's landing court.
The overhead smash is the most powerful attacking shot designed to end rallies quickly.
Clear shots are pushed high and deep into the opponent's backcourt boundary line.
Drop shots require soft touch to guide the shuttlecock closely over the tape.
Drive shots are flat, fast exchanges hit horizontally across the top of the net mesh.
The Badminton World Federation acts as the official global administrative governing agency.
Elite team tournaments include the Thomas Cup for men and the Uber Cup for women.
Players require exceptional agility, rapid lateral footwork, and lightning reflexes to win.
Faults are declared if a player touches the net with their body or racket during play.
Let calls reset a live point if an unforeseen court disruption or asset collision occurs.
